Skill or sugar pill?

Twenty popular skills, each tested with the skill, with a same-length fake text and with nothing. Pick what you want help with and see what each did.

A skill is a short instruction file that tells an AI coding assistant how you want a job done. For each skill here, Driftproof gave a model three text tasks, and each task three ways: with the skill, with a fake text of exactly the same length made of unrelated sentences, and with nothing added. A judge model scored every answer. The fake is the sugar pill: it is there to show whether the skill's own words did something that any text of that length would not.

What the words mean

Against the fake, each word stands for one reading of the receipt reader:

beat the fake
On at least one task it scored above the fake, and on none below.
below the fake
On at least one task it scored below the fake.
too few answers to tell
There were too few answers, or they varied too much, to tell it from the fake.

Against nothing, the words are the receipt reader's too:

Clearly helped
on at least one test task, the average with the skill was higher, the two ranges of scores (the average plus or minus one spread) did not overlap, and the gap was at least 0.05.
Too few answers to tell
there were too few answers, or they varied too much, to call it either way. It does not mean the skill does nothing.
Clearly hurt
on at least one test task, the average with the skill was lower, the two ranges of scores (the average plus or minus one spread) did not overlap, and the gap was at least 0.05.
No verdict, for a router
The receipt reads No verdict: delegated skill not tested. These text tasks test the router's written response, not the delegated skill. Report 015 Amendment 1.

The groups below are a way in, not a finding: each skill sits under what its own description says it does, and skills are listed alphabetically within a group.
