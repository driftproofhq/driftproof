What a sugar pill is here

In medicine a sugar pill is a placebo: it looks like the medicine and does nothing, so a trial can tell what the medicine itself adds. Driftproof does the same with a skill: the model answers each task with the skill and again with a fake of the same length, a few harmless sentences about a market town, so a report can tell what the skill's own words add.

The cards below are the model's answers from the fake side of Reports 017 and 018. Often the model just does the task. Sometimes it says, in so many words, that the text it was handed describes a town and has nothing to do with the job. That is the sugar pill being noticed.

## The plan's biggest weakness

**1. Why "all" and why "next week"?**
Billing jobs differ in kind. Invoice generation, usage rollups, dunning/retries, and renewals have different failure costs. Is there a deadline (contract, vendor shutdown, audit) forcing the date, or is it a target?
*Lean: migrate one low-risk job first, run it in parallel with the cron version, and move the rest only after reconciliation matches.*

## The three undecided items

**2. Duplicate handling**
- Is delivery at-least-once? If so, what is the idempotency key for each billing action (invoice ID, customer+period, charge attempt)?
- Where is the dedupe record stored, and is it written in the same transaction as the side effect?
- What happens if the payment provider charges successfully but your ack fails?
- A cron job runs once per window by construction. Which job relied on that implicitly (e.g. "run at 00:00 for yesterday's usage")?

*Lean: this is the blocker. Without a per-action idempotency key, double-charging is the failure mode, and it's the one customers notice. Decide it before writing any consumer.*

**3. Rollback**
- Once an event-driven job has emitted or consumed events, can you switch back to cron without double-processing or skipping a window?
- Can events be replayed or discarded safely? Are any side effects (charges, emails, ledger entries) non-reversible?
- Who decides to roll back, on what signal, and how fast?

*Lean: keep cron jobs in place but disabled behind a flag, with a cutover marker (last processed period) so either system can resume exactly where the other stopped.*

**4. Migration budget**
- Budget in what: engineering days, infra cost, or tolerated billing error?
- Does it cover running both systems in parallel, building reconciliation, and on-call during the cutover?
- If the budget runs out at 60% done, is a half-migrated billing system acceptable?

*Lean: with no number, "all jobs in a week" can't be judged feasible. Set one, including the parallel-run period.*

## Gaps you didn't mention

- **Ordering and timing:** Do any jobs depend on others finishing first (usage rollup before invoicing)? Events don't give you that for free.
- **Missed triggers:** Cron fails loudly when it doesn't run. What alerts you when an event never arrives?
- **Observability and reconciliation:** How will you prove next month's invoices match what cron would have produced?
- **Backfill and month-end:** Does next week overlap a billing cycle boundary? That is the worst week to cut over.
- **Ownership:** Who is on call, and does finance or support need to sign off?

## What I need back for round 2

1. Is the date fixed, and by what?
2. Is delivery at-least-once, and what's the idempotency key per job type?
3. Can cron stay as a fallback during cutover?

# Reservation design interview

## What I take from your facts

- A **hold** is temporary and lasts 10 minutes.
- **Capture** (taking payment) happens only at **confirmation**, never at hold time.
- **"Booking"** currently means both a hold and a confirmed reservation. This is the biggest risk, because "cancel a booking" or "booking expired" can mean different things depending on who is speaking.

My recommendation: retire "booking" as a domain term. Use **Hold** for the temporary claim and **Reservation** for the confirmed one. Keep "booking" only as informal or UI copy, if at all.

## Interview questions

I've put my suggested default after each question, so you can answer with "yes" or "no, because...".

**Terminology**
1. Is a confirmed reservation the same entity as the hold that preceded it (a status change), or a new entity created from it? *Default: one entity with a status, unless holds and reservations have different lifecycles or data.*
2. Do users, support staff, and the API all use the same words? *Default: one glossary, with UI copy allowed to differ but mapped explicitly.*

**Hold lifecycle**
3. Does the 10 minutes start at creation or at the first user action? Is it fixed, or can it be extended or refreshed? *Default: starts at creation, fixed, no extension.*
4. Who enforces expiry: a timer or job, a TTL in the store, or a check at read time? What happens if confirmation arrives at 10:00.5? *Default: the server's clock is authoritative, and expiry is checked at confirmation as well as by a sweeper.*
5. When a hold expires, is inventory released immediately? Is the expired hold kept for audit? *Default: release immediately, retain the record with status `expired`.*
6. Can one user hold the same inventory twice, or hold many items at once? Is there a cap? *Default: a per-user limit.*

**Confirmation and capture**
7. Capture happens only at confirmation. Is that authorize-then-capture in a single step, or is there a separate authorization earlier? *Default: no payment interaction before confirmation.*
8. If capture fails, what state is the hold in? Does the hold survive for a retry until it expires? *Default: it stays `held` until expiry, and the user can retry.*
9. If capture succeeds but the hold expired a moment earlier (a race), who wins? *Default: capture is only attempted on a live hold, with an atomic transition under a lock or conditional write.*
10. Is confirmation idempotent? What happens on a double click or a client retry? *Default: yes, keyed by an idempotency key.*

**After confirmation**
11. Can a confirmed reservation be cancelled or modified? Are refunds in scope? *Default: out of scope for this design, but recorded as an open question.*

**Operations**
12. What should be logged or metered (holds created, expired, converted, capture failures)? Are conversion rate and expiry rate tracked?

## Proposed documentation

Keep it small, and put each decision in one place.

1. **`docs/glossary.md`** (or `CONTEXT.md`)
   - Definitions for Hold, Reservation, Confirmation, Capture, Expiry, and Release.
   - A "Do not use" entry for *booking*, with a note on which term to use instead.
   - Mapping from each domain term to code identifiers, API fields, and UI copy.

2. **`docs/adr/0001-hold-vs-reservation-terminology.md`**
   - Records the decision to split "booking" into two terms, why, and the migration plan for existing names.
   - Format: Context, Decision, Consequences, Status.

3. **`docs/adr/0002-hold-duration-and-expiry.md`**
   - The 10-minute duration, when the clock starts, who enforces expiry, and the race with confirmation.

4. **`docs/adr/0003-capture-only-at-confirmation.md`**
   - Why no payment interaction happens at hold time, the failure behavior, and idempotency.

5. **`docs/reservation-lifecycle.md`**
   - A state diagram: `held → confirmed`, `held → expired`, `held → released` (user abandons), plus the transitions allowed and what triggers each.
   - The invariants, for example "capture occurs only on the `held → confirmed` transition."

6. **Code-level anchors**
   - Rename types and fields to match the glossary (`Hold`, `Reservation`), or add a comment on any legacy `booking` name pointing to the glossary.
   - Tests named after the invariants, such as "capture is never called for an expired hold."

7. **`docs/open-questions.md`**
   - Unresolved items from the interview (cancellation, refunds, extensions), each with an owner, so the gaps are visible instead of implicit.

## Design

There are two separate questions, so the demo has two parts.

1. **Static reachability.** Treat the transition table as a graph and search for any path that visits `cancelled` and later reaches `paid`. This catches table mistakes, such as a `cancelled -> paid` edge or a path through `cancelled -> pending`.
2. **Interleaving races.** Real systems usually reach `paid` after `cancelled` because a payment was already in flight when the cancel landed. Model payment as two events, `pay_started` and `pay_settled`, and explore every ordering of the events with a bounded depth. Then check whether the `pay_settled` handler guards against a cancelled reservation.

## Sketch (Python, stdlib only, about 40 lines)

    ("paid",      "cancel"):      "refunding",
    ("refunding", "refunded"):    "cancelled",

    if event == "pay_started" and state == "pending":

- GSAP tracks `xPercent`, `yPercent`, `x`, and `y` as separate values and builds the final transform from all of them, so nothing is clobbered between ticks.

## The ownership problem

- The clip has its own timed lifecycle: it shows at its start and runs for its duration.

1. **Preferred: derive it from the playhead.** On every time update, including seeks, compute `visible = start <= t < end`. Apply that value to the clip and remove the separate "hide at end" write. Any seek, forward or backward, then lands on the correct state.

Use atomic rules for small, independent constraints that can be applied anywhere, such as "keep text within the safe margin" or "use the brand palette for accents." Each rule is self-contained, can be tested alone, and can be combined with others.

gsap.set(".centered-heading", { xPercent: -50, yPercent: -50 });
